Chimney Leak Repair in San Francisco, CA

Chimney leaks are one of the most common and most destructive problems facing San Francisco homeowners. AL Chimney Service diagnoses and repairs all sources of chimney water infiltration.

Call +1 925-433-7889 — Available 24/7

Common Sources of Chimney Leaks

Damaged Chimney Flashing

Flashing is the metal sealing system at the junction of the chimney and roof. Failed or improperly installed flashing is the most common cause of chimney leaks. We repair and replace all types of chimney flashing including step flashing, counter flashing, and saddle flashing.

Cracked Chimney Crown

A cracked crown allows rainwater to enter at the top of the chimney structure. See our Chimney Crown Repair service.

Missing or Damaged Chimney Cap

Without a cap, rain falls directly into the flue opening. A properly fitted cap is essential during San Francisco's rainy season.

Deteriorated Mortar Joints

Porous or eroded mortar absorbs rainfall and allows water to migrate through the chimney structure. Repointing restores the waterproof barrier.

Condensation Inside the Flue

High-efficiency appliances produce flue gases cool enough to condense inside an oversized flue. This "phantom leak" is resolved by proper relining.

Our Leak Diagnosis Process

We conduct a systematic inspection to identify every source of moisture intrusion before recommending repairs. We use water testing, video scanning, and thermal imaging where necessary to pinpoint the exact location and cause of each leak.

Tracing the Source of a Chimney Leak — Why Diagnosis Comes First

Here's something that surprises a lot of homeowners: a chimney leak that shows up as a stain on the ceiling near your fireplace might actually be coming from four or five different possible locations on or around the chimney. Water is devious — it enters at one point and travels along framing, masonry, and insulation before showing up somewhere completely different. Patching the wrong thing is a waste of money and leaves the actual problem in place, leaking through another winter.

This is why the first step in any chimney leak repair is thorough diagnosis. We don't start replacing flashing the moment we see a water stain. We trace the water path systematically, using a combination of visual inspection, hands-on testing, video scanning, and in some cases thermal imaging. Only once we understand exactly where and how water is entering do we recommend and perform repairs. This approach costs more time upfront but saves money overall — because we fix the right things, and we fix them once.

How Much Water Damage Can a Chimney Leak Really Cause?

A lot of homeowners see a drip or a small stain and figure it can wait. We understand — in a city as expensive as San Francisco, every repair dollar gets scrutinized. But chimney leaks are one of those problems where early action is dramatically cheaper than delayed action. Let's be specific about what water damage looks like when a chimney leak is left unaddressed:

  • Rusted damper assembly: The damper sits in the smoke chamber, directly in the path of water entering through a missing cap or cracked crown. A rusted damper no longer seals, letting cold drafts into your home year-round and cutting heating efficiency. Replacement costs $300–$600.
  • Deteriorated flue liner: Water cycling through the flue liner cracks tiles and erodes mortar. A cracked liner is not just a repair issue — it's a safety hazard, as combustion gases can escape through the liner into your home's structure. Relining a damaged flue costs $2,000–$5,000.
  • Firebox and smoke chamber breakdown: The refractory mortar and firebrick in the firebox are designed to handle heat, not sustained moisture. Water causes the mortar to crumble and the bricks to spall. Rebuilding a firebox is a significant masonry job, often $1,500–$3,000 or more.
  • Structural framing damage: When water penetrates past the chimney and reaches the wood framing of your home — rafters, joists, wall studs — you're now looking at a general contractor repair on top of the chimney repair. This is where costs can spiral into five figures.
  • Mold: San Francisco's persistently cool, damp climate means that moisture-saturated materials inside walls and ceilings can develop mold within weeks. Mold remediation in a Bay Area home is not inexpensive.

The arithmetic is straightforward: a flashing repair costs a few hundred dollars. Waiting until that flashing failure has saturated the framing above your fireplace costs thousands. If you're seeing any sign of moisture, the right time to call is today.

The 5 Most Common Chimney Leak Sources in San Francisco Homes

After years of diagnosing and repairing chimney leaks throughout San Francisco, we see the same problems repeating themselves across the city's housing stock. Here are the five most common sources we find:

1. Failed Chimney Flashing

Flashing is the metal waterproofing system at the junction where your chimney meets the roof. It consists of multiple overlapping pieces — step flashing along the sides of the chimney, counter flashing embedded into the mortar joints, and often a saddle or cricket behind the chimney to divert water away. When flashing fails — due to age, improper installation, shifting from minor earthquakes, or thermal expansion cycling — the roof-chimney junction becomes a funnel for rainwater directly into the structure below.

This is the single most common cause of chimney leaks in San Francisco, and it's one we see on a huge percentage of the Victorian and Edwardian homes throughout the city. The original flashing on a 100-year-old SF home is almost certainly at the end of its life. Even newer flashing that was improperly installed (inadequate overlap, wrong materials, missing counter flashing) fails prematurely.

2. Cracked or Deteriorated Chimney Crown

As discussed in detail on our chimney crown repair page, a damaged crown is a major water entry point at the very top of the chimney structure. Cracks in the crown allow water to enter the masonry directly, bypassing the cap entirely. This is particularly common in older San Francisco homes where the original crown was built with a weak mortar mix rather than proper concrete.

3. Missing or Damaged Chimney Cap

Without a cap, every rain event deposits water directly into the flue opening. The top of the flue is at the highest and most exposed point of your chimney, and direct rain entry saturates the entire length of the flue system. Strong Bay Area winds can also dislodge or damage caps, leaving flues suddenly exposed. If your cap is gone or damaged going into the rainy season, this is an emergency.

4. Eroded Mortar Joints

The mortar between your chimney bricks is not permanent. In San Francisco's wet climate, mortar erodes at an accelerating rate as it ages. Once mortar recedes below the face of the bricks — a condition called joint erosion or weathering — the bricks themselves are no longer properly bonded, and the mortar bed becomes a network of channels that carry water deep into the chimney structure. Tuckpointing (removing and replacing eroded mortar) restores the waterproof barrier and is far less expensive than repairing the cascade of damage that eroded joints cause.

5. Condensation Inside the Flue (The "Phantom Leak")

This one confuses homeowners, because the water appears to be coming from inside the chimney rather than outside. High-efficiency gas furnaces and boilers produce flue gases that are cool enough to condense inside an oversized liner — and many older San Francisco homes that have updated their heating systems now have a modern high-efficiency appliance venting through an old, oversized masonry flue that it was never designed for. The cool gases condense on the liner walls and drip down into the firebox, looking for all the world like a roof leak. The fix is not patching anything on the exterior — it's properly lining the flue with an appropriately sized liner for the appliance.

Emergency Leak Response — When You Can't Wait

Some chimney leaks are slow-developing problems that can be scheduled for repair in the next week or two. Others are urgent. If you're seeing any of the following, call us right away at +1 925-433-7889 — we're available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week:

  • Active dripping into the firebox or onto the hearth during a rain event
  • Water stains appearing or expanding on ceilings or walls in real time
  • A cap that has been blown off or visibly damaged by wind, leaving the flue completely open
  • Any sign of water near electrical outlets, wiring, or light fixtures adjacent to the chimney area
  • Water pooling on the hearth or in the firebox ash pit

For active leaks during rain, we can provide emergency response and temporary protection to stop the immediate water intrusion, followed by permanent repair once conditions allow.

Long-Term Leak Prevention — Protecting Your Chimney After the Repair

Fixing the immediate leak is step one. Step two is making sure it doesn't happen again. After we've diagnosed and repaired the source of your chimney leak, we'll walk you through a recommended maintenance plan to keep your chimney protected long-term. This typically includes:

  1. Chimney cap inspection and replacement if needed — the most cost-effective single defense against water entry
  2. Crown inspection and coating — sealing the crown surface against moisture infiltration
  3. Tuckpointing of eroded mortar joints — restoring the masonry's waterproof barrier
  4. Professional waterproofing treatment — applying a breathable, vapor-permeable sealant to the exterior masonry to repel water while letting the structure breathe
  5. Annual inspection schedule — catching new issues before they become leaks

This comprehensive approach to moisture management is the reason some San Francisco chimneys last 100+ years in excellent condition while others deteriorate in 20. The difference is proactive maintenance, and it doesn't have to be expensive when problems are caught early.
