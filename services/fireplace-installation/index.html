Fireplace Installation Options

Gas Fireplace Inserts

Gas fireplace inserts are installed into existing masonry fireplaces, converting them into highly efficient gas-burning units. They require a new stainless steel flue liner and a gas supply connection. AL Chimney Service handles the complete installation including liner, surround, and gas hookup coordination.

New Gas Fireplaces

Direct-vent gas fireplaces can be installed in almost any room without an existing chimney. They vent through a small double-wall pipe through an exterior wall or roof. We install all leading gas fireplace brands and manage permit applications with the City of San Francisco.

Wood-Burning Fireplace Inserts

High-efficiency EPA-certified wood stove inserts provide dramatically more heat output than open fireplaces while using less wood. Installation requires liner replacement and surround fitting.

Electric Fireplaces

Electric fireplace inserts provide the ambiance of a fireplace with no chimney requirements. We install electric units in existing masonry or new built-in applications.

San Francisco Permit Information

All new fireplace installations in San Francisco require building permits. Wood-burning appliances are subject to air quality regulations under the Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD). Gas fireplace installations require gas permits and inspection. We guide you through the permit process for every installation.

Your Options for a New Fireplace in San Francisco

Adding a fireplace to a San Francisco home is absolutely achievable — even if you don't have an existing chimney, even if you're in a condo, and even if your home is a historic Victorian with limited wall space. The range of options available today means that almost any home can accommodate some type of fireplace. The real question is which type makes the most sense for your space, your lifestyle, and your budget.

San Francisco is a city that genuinely needs fireplaces. Even in summer, fog keeps the evenings cool. In winter, the combination of damp air and temperatures in the 40s and 50s makes a fire feel essential rather than decorative. A well-chosen fireplace installation doesn't just add warmth — it transforms how you use a room and meaningfully increases your home's value.

Wood-Burning vs Gas vs Electric: Which Is Right for You?

Wood-Burning Fireplaces and Inserts

Wood-burning fireplaces provide the most authentic fire experience — the crackling sound, the real wood smell, the radiant heat that warms you from across the room. For many San Francisco homeowners, especially those in historic homes with existing masonry chimneys, a high-efficiency wood-burning insert is the ideal choice. Modern EPA-certified inserts are dramatically more efficient than open masonry fireplaces, converting up to 75–80% of the wood's energy into usable heat compared to just 10–15% for an open fireplace.

That said, wood burning in San Francisco comes with meaningful restrictions. The Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD) enforces Spare the Air alerts throughout the winter season, and during those alerts, wood burning is prohibited. If you use your fireplace frequently during winter evenings and don't want to be shut out on cold nights, a wood-burning appliance might frustrate you more than it satisfies you. If you use it occasionally and primarily for ambiance, it may be perfect.

Gas Fireplaces and Inserts

Gas fireplaces are by far the most popular choice for San Francisco homeowners, and it's easy to see why. They turn on with a remote or a wall switch, they heat a room within minutes, they're not subject to Spare the Air restrictions, and they require far less maintenance than wood-burning systems. Modern gas fireplaces feature realistic ceramic log sets, lifelike flame patterns, and efficient heat output that can meaningfully reduce your reliance on central heating.

Gas fireplace inserts are installed into existing masonry fireboxes, converting an inefficient open fireplace into a sealed, high-output gas appliance. New direct-vent gas fireplaces can be installed in homes without any existing chimney — they vent through a small coaxial pipe that goes through an exterior wall or through the roof, so there's no need to build a full masonry chimney.

Electric Fireplaces

Electric fireplaces have improved enormously in recent years. While they don't provide the same radiant heat as a wood or gas unit, high-quality electric inserts produce convincing flame effects and supplement room heating effectively. They require no venting whatsoever, which makes them ideal for condos, rooms on interior walls, or any space where running a gas line or vent pipe isn't practical. Installation is straightforward, and they can even be used in summer for the ambiance without any heat output.

San Francisco Building Code and Air Quality Rules

This is where things get specific to San Francisco, and it's important to understand the regulatory environment before you commit to an installation. We guide all our customers through the permit process, but you should know what's involved upfront.

  • Building permits: All new fireplace installations in San Francisco require a building permit from the San Francisco Department of Building Inspection (SFDBI). This includes gas inserts installed into existing fireplaces, new direct-vent gas units, and electric built-ins that involve any structural modification.
  • Gas permits: Any work on a gas line — including connecting a gas fireplace to your home's gas supply — requires a separate gas permit and inspection by a licensed plumber or gas fitter. We coordinate with licensed gas contractors for all our gas fireplace installations.
  • BAAQMD regulations: The Bay Area Air Quality Management District restricts wood-burning on Spare the Air days, which can occur frequently from November through February. New wood-burning fireplaces must meet EPA emission standards. If you're replacing an existing fireplace, BAAQMD may require the existing appliance to be removed or rendered inoperable.
  • Seismic considerations: Any new masonry construction — including a masonry fireplace — must meet current California seismic codes, which require steel reinforcement and specific mortar and anchoring specifications. This adds cost compared to other states but is non-negotiable in the Bay Area.

We handle the permit applications, coordinate inspections, and make sure every installation is fully code-compliant. Working with a contractor who knows the San Francisco permit process inside out saves you weeks of delays and the risk of failed inspections.

Zero-Clearance Fireplaces: The Modern Solution

Zero-clearance fireplaces are prefabricated metal fireplace units designed to be installed directly against combustible walls and framing — hence the name "zero clearance." They're one of the most versatile options available because they don't require existing masonry or a chase, and they can be framed into almost any wall in almost any room.

Zero-clearance units are available in both wood-burning and gas configurations. The gas versions are among the most efficient home heating appliances available, with some models rated at over 80% AFUE (Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency). For San Francisco homes being renovated or for additions where a fireplace is desired but no chimney exists, a zero-clearance gas fireplace with a direct-vent termination through an exterior wall is often the fastest and most cost-effective path to a real, functional fireplace.

The framed chase can be finished with tile, stone, brick veneer, wood, or any other non-combustible surround material to match your interior. This flexibility makes zero-clearance fireplaces especially popular in San Francisco's contemporary renovations and in Edwardian-era homes that have been updated with modern interiors.

What the Installation Process Looks Like

We know that "getting a fireplace" can feel like a huge, complicated project. Here's the step-by-step reality so you know exactly what to expect when you work with AL Chimney Service:

  1. Consultation and site visit: We visit your home, assess the space, discuss your goals, and recommend the right type of fireplace for your situation. We look at gas line access, venting options, wall construction, and any existing fireplace or chimney.
  2. Proposal and permitting: We provide a detailed written proposal and initiate the permit process with SFDBI. For gas installations, we coordinate with a licensed gas contractor for the supply connection.
  3. Framing and rough-in: For new installations, we frame the chase, prepare the venting pathway, and handle any structural work. For inserts, this step is minimal.
  4. Fireplace unit installation: We install the fireplace unit, connect all venting, and prepare the surround area for finishing.
  5. Gas connection and testing: The licensed gas contractor connects the gas supply and we test the appliance for proper operation, draft, and safety.
  6. Inspection and sign-off: City inspectors review the installation. We're present for all inspections and handle any required adjustments.
  7. Finishing: We complete the surround installation, clean up, and walk you through operating your new fireplace.
