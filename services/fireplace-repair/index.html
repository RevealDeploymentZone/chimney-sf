Common Fireplace Repairs

Refractory Panel Replacement

Prefabricated metal fireplaces use refractory panels (also called firebox panels) on the side, rear, and floor of the firebox. These panels crack with age and thermal cycling. Cracked panels must be replaced to maintain fire containment. We stock panels for all major fireplace brands and can custom-cut refractory material for older models.

Firebox Rebuilding

Masonry fireplaces with extensively damaged firebrick or mortar require partial or complete firebox rebuilding. We use refractory mortar and heat-rated firebrick to restore the firebox to safe operating condition.

Glass Door Repair & Replacement

Cracked or damaged fireplace glass doors compromise safety and appearance. We replace glass panels and repair door frames for both masonry and prefabricated fireplaces.

Gas Valve & Igniter Service

Gas fireplaces with malfunctioning valves, igniters, or thermopiles require service by qualified technicians. We service all major gas fireplace brands and replace components as needed.

Damper Repair

Stuck or broken dampers prevent proper operation and allow heat and cold air to enter the home. We repair and replace throat dampers and install top-sealing dampers.

Understanding Your Firebox: What Can Go Wrong

The firebox is the heart of your fireplace — it's the chamber where the fire actually burns. Think of it as an engineered system designed to contain extreme heat, direct combustion gases upward, and protect your home's framing from fire. When any part of that system fails, you're not just looking at an ugly repair — you could be looking at a genuine safety hazard.

In San Francisco, we see firebox damage accelerated by a few local factors. The city's famous fog and marine layer keep moisture levels high year-round. That humidity works its way into the masonry through the chimney opening, through cracked crowns, and through joints that have lost their mortar. When that moisture-laden masonry experiences the intense heat of a fire, it expands and contracts at a rate the material wasn't designed to handle. Over time, that constant cycling causes the mortar joints between firebricks to crack and crumble, and eventually the bricks themselves can crack or shift.

In prefabricated metal fireplaces — which are extremely common in San Francisco condos, Edwardian flats, and post-war homes — the firebox is lined with modular refractory panels rather than hand-laid firebrick. These panels are designed to be replaceable, which is good news. But many homeowners don't realize the panels have a limited lifespan and need to be inspected annually. A panel with a hairline crack might look fine until the crack widens during a fire and allows heat to transfer directly to the metal firebox shell and the adjacent framing.

The Safety Risk of a Damaged Firebox

Let's be direct here: using a fireplace with a damaged firebox is dangerous. It's not a matter of aesthetics or efficiency — it's a fire and carbon monoxide risk. Here's what can happen when damage goes unaddressed:

  • House fire: Cracked refractory panels or missing firebrick allow heat to reach combustible framing behind the fireplace. A fire that seems completely contained in the firebox can be slowly igniting the wall structure behind it.
  • Carbon monoxide intrusion: Gaps in the firebox allow combustion gases — including carbon monoxide — to leak into living spaces rather than drafting safely up the flue.
  • Chimney fire: Damaged smoke chambers and shelves allow creosote to accumulate in areas it shouldn't, creating fuel for a chimney fire that can spread to the roof.
  • Structural deterioration: Water entering through a compromised firebox can damage the surrounding masonry, the floor, and even the foundation — especially relevant in San Francisco's older building stock.

If you've noticed white staining (efflorescence) on your fireplace surround, a persistent smell even when the fireplace isn't in use, or any visible cracking in the back or sides of the firebox, call us before you light another fire. A professional inspection takes about an hour and gives you definitive answers.

Refractory Panels vs Firebrick Repairs

This is one of the most common questions we get: "Do I need new panels or does the whole firebox need to be rebuilt?" The answer depends entirely on what type of fireplace you have and how extensive the damage is.

Refractory Panel Replacement

Prefabricated metal fireplaces use modular refractory panels — typically 1 to 2 inches thick — that line the back and sides of the firebox. These panels are rated for specific fireplace models and need to be replaced with the correct specification. We stock panels for all major manufacturers including Heatilator, Heat & Glo, Majestic, Superior, and many others. For discontinued models or unusual sizes, we can custom-cut refractory board to fit. Panel replacement is a relatively straightforward repair — expect it to take a few hours and cost a fraction of what a full firebox rebuild would run. Most panel replacements in San Francisco homes run in the range of $200–$600 depending on the number of panels and panel availability.

Firebrick and Refractory Mortar Repair

Masonry fireplaces use real firebrick — a dense, heat-rated brick — set in refractory mortar. This mortar is specifically engineered to withstand repeated heating and cooling cycles, but it does degrade over decades of use. When joints fail, individual bricks may shift or fall entirely. Minor repairs involve grinding out the failed joint, cleaning the surface, and repacking with fresh refractory mortar. More significant damage requires replacing individual bricks or entire sections of the firebox floor, sides, or rear wall. In the most severe cases, the entire firebox is rebuilt from scratch — but that's usually reserved for chimneys where water damage has been allowed to progress for many years without attention.

Damper Repair: Why It Matters More Than You Think

Your fireplace damper is a plate or valve that sits at the throat of the chimney (just above the firebox) or at the very top of the chimney (a top-sealing damper). When you're not using the fireplace, the damper should be closed. When you light a fire, you open it to allow smoke to draft up and out. Sounds simple — but a failing damper causes a whole cascade of problems.

In San Francisco's often-cool climate, an open or broken damper is essentially leaving a hole in your roof. During those summer fog months and the rainy season from October through April, outside air — and moisture — pour straight down your chimney and into your living room. Homeowners often notice cold drafts near the fireplace in winter and attribute it to drafty old windows. Sometimes it's the damper. Beyond comfort, moisture entering through an open damper year after year accelerates deterioration of the firebox, the smoke shelf, and the flue liner.

Throat dampers — the traditional cast-iron plate style — are prone to warping from heat and corroding from moisture. If your damper won't open fully, won't stay open, or won't seal completely, it needs to be repaired or replaced. We repair and replace throat dampers, and we also install top-sealing dampers, which seal at the top of the chimney and provide dramatically better energy performance than a traditional throat damper.

Is Your Fireplace Repairable or Does It Need Rebuilding?

This is the real question, and we understand that homeowners want a straight answer — not a runaround. Here's how we think about it:

A fireplace is worth repairing when the firebox structure is fundamentally sound, the damage is localized to specific components (panels, a section of brick, a damper, glass), and the repair will restore the fireplace to a fully safe and functional condition. The vast majority of fireplaces we see in San Francisco homes fall into this category. A repair is almost always more economical and just as durable as a replacement.

A fireplace may need partial or full rebuilding when water damage has been allowed to progress for many years, when the masonry has structurally failed across a large portion of the firebox, or when an old fireplace was built to codes that are no longer acceptable for safe operation. We'll always give you an honest assessment — and if a repair will do the job, we'll repair it. We're not in the business of upselling you a full rebuild when a $300 panel replacement solves the problem.

  1. We perform a thorough visual inspection of the firebox, panels, mortar joints, damper, smoke shelf, and firebox opening
  2. We photograph all damage and identify the root cause (not just the symptom)
  3. We give you a clear written estimate with options where applicable
  4. We complete the repair using appropriate materials — no generic big-box store mortar or incorrect panel substitutions
  5. We test the fireplace after repair and confirm the damper operation, draft, and overall condition before we leave
