Annual Wood Stove Maintenance: What's Involved

A lot of San Francisco homeowners assume that if their wood stove is burning and producing heat, it must be fine. That's a risky assumption. Wood stoves accumulate creosote — a tar-like, highly flammable byproduct of incomplete combustion — inside the stovepipe and flue liner every single time they're used. Over a full burning season, that buildup reaches dangerous levels that no amount of hot fires will safely burn away on their own.

Annual maintenance by a certified chimney sweep is the only reliable way to keep your wood stove operating safely and efficiently. When AL Chimney Service visits your San Francisco home for a wood stove service, here's what we cover:

  • Visual and video inspection of the flue liner from top to bottom
  • Sweeping and removal of all creosote and soot deposits from the flue
  • Inspection and cleaning of the stovepipe (connector pipe) sections
  • Checking all stovepipe joints and seams for gaps or deterioration
  • Inspection of the firebox, including the floor, walls, and baffle plate
  • Testing of air controls, damper, and draft regulation
  • Door gasket inspection — a compromised gasket allows uncontrolled air into the firebox, reducing efficiency and increasing creosote production
  • Catalytic combustor check (if applicable) — catalyst elements degrade over time and need cleaning or replacement
  • Chimney cap and spark arrestor inspection
  • Assessment of clearances around the stove, hearth pad, and wall protections

The whole service typically takes 1 to 2 hours depending on the condition of your system. We leave your home clean — we use drop cloths and a HEPA vacuum to contain soot and debris. Most homeowners are surprised by how thorough the process is compared to a quick sweep.

Wood Stove Safety in San Francisco Homes

San Francisco has a unique housing stock — a lot of the homes here are Victorian and Edwardian-era buildings, many of which were retrofitted with wood stoves in the 1970s and 1980s during the energy crisis. That means there are thousands of stoves in the city that are 30, 40, even 50 years old. Some were installed properly; many were not.

The most serious wood stove hazards we encounter in San Francisco homes include:

  • Inadequate clearances: Many older installations don't meet current minimum clearance requirements between the stove and combustible walls or cabinets. This is a direct fire risk.
  • Deteriorated flue liners: Clay tile liners crack over time from thermal cycling. A cracked liner allows hot combustion gases and sparks to contact the surrounding framing — a leading cause of house fires.
  • Corroded stovepipe: Single-wall stovepipe rusts and corrodes, especially in San Francisco's coastal fog environment. We frequently find pipes with pinhole leaks or joint failures leaking carbon monoxide into living spaces.
  • Improper installation of ovens or inserts: Some homeowners have added cooking accessories or inserts without proper liner connections, creating smoke and CO hazards.
  • Blocked flues: Birds, animals, and debris can block flues, causing carbon monoxide to back-draft into the home instead of exhausting outside.

If you haven't had your wood stove and chimney professionally inspected in the last year — or if you've never had it done — please call us before your next fire. The cost of an inspection and cleaning is a fraction of what chimney fire remediation or carbon monoxide exposure costs a family.

California EPA Wood Stove Regulations

California has some of the strictest wood-burning appliance regulations in the country, and the Bay Area adds another layer on top of state rules through the Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD).

Here's what San Francisco wood stove owners need to know:

  • EPA Phase 2 certification required: Any new wood stove installed in California must meet EPA Phase 2 emission standards, which limit particulate matter output to 2.0 grams per hour or less. Many older stoves in use today do not meet this standard.
  • Spare the Air Alerts: The BAAQMD issues Spare the Air alerts during periods of poor air quality, typically in winter. On Spare the Air days, wood burning is prohibited throughout the nine-county Bay Area — including San Francisco. Violations carry fines up to $500 per day.
  • No burning of prohibited materials: California law prohibits burning treated wood, painted wood, garbage, plastic, cardboard, and other materials in wood stoves. Only dry, seasoned firewood is permitted.
  • Change-out programs: BAAQMD periodically offers financial incentives for homeowners to replace older, non-certified wood stoves and fireplaces with cleaner burning alternatives. We can advise you on current program availability.

We help all our San Francisco clients understand their obligations under these rules and make sure their equipment is compliant. If your stove is an older, non-certified model, we can walk you through your options — including what it takes to bring your installation up to current standards or make the switch to a cleaner appliance.

Common Wood Stove Problems We Fix

Over years of servicing wood stoves across San Francisco and the Bay Area, our technicians have seen nearly every problem these appliances can develop. Here are the issues we encounter most frequently:

Smoking Into the Room

A wood stove that releases smoke into the living space rather than drawing it up the flue is one of the most common complaints we receive. The causes range from a cold flue that needs priming, to a blocked chimney cap, to an undersized flue liner, to negative pressure inside a tightly sealed modern home. We diagnose the specific cause and fix it — because "cracking a window" is not a real solution.

Poor Draft and Slow Burning

If your fires are sluggish and hard to sustain, the problem is usually a dirty or restricted flue, a damaged baffle, or a faulty air control. Sometimes it's as simple as wet wood. We assess the full system to find the real cause.

Creosote Glazing

Stage 3 creosote — the shiny, tar-like variety — is a serious fire hazard and requires specialized chemical treatment before mechanical removal. Standard brushing won't touch it. If we find glazed creosote during your inspection, we'll explain the treatment process and what it costs before proceeding.

Cracked Firebricks and Refractory Panels

The firebox interior is lined with refractory materials designed to withstand intense heat. Over time these crack and spall. Minor cracks can be repaired with refractory cement; more extensive damage requires panel replacement. This is not cosmetic — a compromised firebox interior allows excessive heat to reach the outer shell of the stove.

Broken or Warped Baffles

The baffle plate at the top of the firebox directs combustion gases and increases combustion efficiency. When it warps or cracks, combustion efficiency drops significantly and you burn more wood for less heat. Baffle replacement is a straightforward repair we can usually complete during a service visit.

Connector Pipes and Clearance Requirements

The connector pipe — the section of stovepipe that connects your wood stove to the chimney flue — is one of the most neglected parts of a wood stove installation. Many homeowners focus their attention on the stove itself or the chimney, but the connector pipe is where problems often hide.

In San Francisco, we regularly find connector pipes that are:

  • Installed without the correct number of screws at each joint section
  • Made from lightweight single-wall pipe in locations where double-wall is required
  • Running too close to combustible materials (the minimum clearance for single-wall pipe to combustibles is 18 inches)
  • Corroded through due to condensation — particularly common in San Francisco's foggy climate
  • Running horizontally for too long a distance, reducing draft and increasing creosote buildup
  • Connected to the wrong size thimble or chimney opening, causing air leakage

We inspect the entire connector pipe run during every service visit, and we replace deteriorated sections on the spot in most cases. Connector pipe is relatively inexpensive — typically $80–$200 for a standard installation — and replacing it is far cheaper than dealing with a chimney fire or CO incident.

Is Your Wood Stove Ready for Burning Season?

In San Francisco, "burning season" officially runs from November 1 through the end of February, though the mild Bay Area climate means many residents use their wood stoves throughout the fall and into spring whenever temperatures drop. The best time to schedule your annual service is in September or October — before demand peaks and before the first cold nights arrive.

Don't wait until the first cold snap to call. Here's what happens when you do:

  1. Every other homeowner in SF is calling at the same time, and appointment availability drops sharply in October and November.
  2. You rush the first fire of the season without knowing the condition of your flue, which is exactly when chimney fires and CO incidents happen.
  3. If repairs are needed — a cracked liner, failed pipe, damaged baffle — there's no buffer time to get parts and complete the work before cold weather hits.

Scheduling in late summer or early fall means you get your preferred appointment time, your stove is ready to go when you need it, and you have peace of mind going into the season.
